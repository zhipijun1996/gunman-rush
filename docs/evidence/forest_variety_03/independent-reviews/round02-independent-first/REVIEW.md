## Design decision and whole-module strategy

**First two are accepted as ordinary same-family connection alternatives only. Do not fill4of6 new main-route slots with them.** Their actual crown, lower outlet and catch topology is the same. The intended HH and RH strings are both optional under real legal alternatives, so counting letters, targets or named functions as new challenge families would repeat the previous error.

Keep the old forest's good dynamic H/R grammar, shared trunk and clear landings. In the new ordinary opening, use one of these small connectors to a safe root and let its legal cheap solution stay. Then choose a different decision: actual upper/lower destination, downward side entry, phase/kill alternative, recoil direction around a trunk, reward behind the departure or a return opening connecting a different known place. Those differences must be visible in real world ports, actor states and rewards, not solely in metadata.

The double-window upper crossing is a good technical candidate for a second family because the complete source offers a distinct lower inward root. Its added value is still conditional on actually crossing the lower window and wiring its exit to a different destination. If the world immediately rejoins both to the same eastward continuation, it loses that value. A stopped two-cell downward elbow could provide a genuinely different direction and tempo once its safe bay-to-second-fruit route works. It should remain optional until actual lower-port recovery/world connection is established.

Do not solve the680wu macro rise by repeatedly raising these receivers. Design2–3 different-purpose loops that reuse old movement, then draw the seed-selected actual adjacency. Use a finite compatible module pool per role and prevent immediate same-family repetition; role selection itself must not force a long chain. Seed changes should include/remove actual roots/fruits/windows and change destinations, enemy/route opportunity or reward position, not only alter test-driver order.
